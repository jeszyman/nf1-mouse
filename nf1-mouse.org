** README

*** Project overview
K08 Aim 2: cfDNA monitoring of tumor biology and therapy response in /in vivo/ NF1 MPNST PDX models.
- Aim 2.1: Correlate cfDNA features with tumor characteristics at terminal bleed
- Aim 2.2: Characterize cfDNA dynamics in serial, non-terminal bleeds
- Aim 2.3: Infer tumor molecular changes from cfDNA dynamics
*** Experimental system
- 6 PDX lines:
  - Chr8q-gain: JH-2-002 (JHU), WU-356 (WashU), MN2 (WashU), WU-225 (WashU)
  - Chr8q-WT: JH-2-055 (JHU), WU-487 (WashU)
- Host strain: NOD.Cg-Rag1^{null} Il2rg^{null} (NRG)
- 5 treatment arms: vehicle, mirdametinib (A, 0.3 mg/kg QDx5 days), volasertib (VS, 50 mg/kg BIDx5 days), mirdametinib + volasertib (A+VS), untreated
- Serial submandibular bleeds (50–100 μL) at baseline and days 7, 14, 21, 28
- Terminal bleed (~400 μL) and tumor harvest at day 28
- cfDNA isolated with MagMax kit, profiled by EM-seq
- EM-seq libraries exist for Chr8q-WT lines only: WU-487 (6 terminal) and JH-2-055 (12 serial). No Chr8q-gain lines have EM-seq data as of [2026-03-18 Wed].

*** NF1 MPNST PDX model system
NF1 (neurofibromatosis type 1) affects ~1 in 2,500 individuals worldwide. Plexiform neurofibromas (PNs) can progress through atypical neurofibroma (AN) to malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor (MPNST), the leading cause of cancer death in NF1. Lifetime MPNST risk is 8–13%; 5-year disease-specific survival is ~30%. Molecular progression follows NF1 biallelic loss → CDKN2A/B deletion → TP53 and PRC2 (SUZ12/EED) loss, with MPNST subtypes distinguished by H3K27me3-loss vs H3K27me3-retained status. Patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models recapitulate tumor histology and molecular features, enabling controlled /in vivo/ studies of cfDNA dynamics.

*** cfDNA as translational platform
cfDNA from PDX mouse plasma provides a real-time, systemic measure of tumor biology:
- Serial submandibular bleeds enable longitudinal monitoring without sacrificing the animal
- Human-specific reads recovered after Disambiguate filtering
- Multi-omic cfDNA features: copy number (ichorCNA), fragmentomics (NNMF signatures), methylation (EM-seq), nucleosome positioning (Griffin)
- Terminal bleeds paired with matched tumor tissue for validation
- Validation chain: terminal bleed validated against final tissue state → final nonterminal bleed validated against terminal bleed → initial nonterminal bleed validated against original PDX state
- Tissue-level orthogonal assays: ATAC-seq and RNA-seq on matched tumor, with possible CUT&RUN follow-up for transcription factor binding

- [[id:57a06d7a-302b-488f-94af-c41a98684555][NF1-associated tissue of origin]] uses cfDNA tissue-of-origin inference across NCI, Hopkins, Mayo, WashU, and U of M samples
** Xengraft model read disambiguation bioinformatics
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       3b09d020-bbe8-4a0e-9379-a6de86d47852
:END:

PDX plasma cfDNA contains a mixture of human tumor-derived and mouse host-derived reads. No established best practice exists for separating species in EM-seq data, where enzymatic C-to-T conversion at unmethylated CpGs precludes direct k-mer classification. Bhandari, et al. benchmarked 12 mouse read removal protocols for PDX WGS/WES/RNA-seq and found Xengsort (k-mer) outperforms alignment-based methods including Disambiguate, but also that "all protocols were effective at removing mouse reads" and "performance difference was minor, usually within 5% of the human reads"  [cite:@bhandari2025]. Xengsort's k-mer identity breaks under bisulfite/EM-seq conversion.

3. Isolate cfDNA - for each isolation, follow the steps of the \citetitle{magmax_manual} \cite{magmax_manual}. (This is the manual specifically for small volume isolations). Use manual protocol, option 2. For 50 μL samples, first dilute sample volume up to 100 μL with PBS.

4. Perform right-sided bead selection as described in \citeauthor{heider2020} \citetitle{heider2020} \cite{heider2020}, methods section "Size Selection and Library Preparation of Blood-Spot Cell-Free DNA" through the 7:1 bead ratio elution:

/We opted to perform size selection to remove contaminating long gDNA fragments that would have prohibited library preparation. Thus, a right-side size selection was performed on DNA eluates using AMPure XP beads (Beckman Coulter). For this purpose, we adapted a protocol for a right-side size selection (13). Following optimization of bead:sample ratios for cfDNA fragment sizes, we used a bead:sample ratio of 1:1 to remove contaminating gDNA. The supernatant was retained as part of the right-side size-selection protocol. A second size-selection step used a 7:1 bead:sample ratio to capture remaining fragments, and the size-selected DNA was eluted in 25 μL water./

The resulting 25 μL volume is appropriate to carry forward for EM-seq library preparation \cite{NEB2024}.

Perform for each analyte *in nonconsecutive duplicate*. One microliter volumes are sufficient for testing. If resulting concentration is <200 pg, re-measure with 3 μL volumes. Retain concentration values and raw flourescence values including standard curve raw fluorescence.
   2. Nanodrop spectrophotometry as described in the user guide \cite{nanodrop}. Use same volume as in the Qubit and again test all samples in nonconsecutive duplicate. Use the dsDNA measurement program and retain ng/uL, A260/A280, A260/A230, and UV spectrum. Also take a A414 reading.
   3. Bioanalyzer electrophoresis with HS kit protocol. Perfrom once per analyte. Retain peak table XML and electropherogram XML.

** Analysis map

*Design principle 1 — canonical path layout with sparse replicas* (a.k.a. namespace-coherent partial mirroring / path-isomorphic subset replication; conceptually a /conformed namespace/ across stores). Every file sits at the same relative path on every store that holds it, but no single store is required to hold the complete tree — each host or bucket contains a subset of the canonical namespace. This makes any analysis script portable across hosts by changing only the root prefix. Implication: when adding a new path, choose its canonical location once and use that exact relative path everywhere it appears; never store the same logical data at different paths on different stores.

*Design principle 2 — immutable raw + per-project inputs duplication for vendor-delivered data* (FASTQs, raw sequencer outputs, third-party deliveries). Every externally delivered file lives at *two* bucket paths, with filenames preserved verbatim between them:
- *Immutable raw delivery*: =gs://chaudhuri-lab-bucket1/jeszyman/data/<vendor-group>/<delivery-name>/<flowcell-or-batch>/<sample-dir>/<filename>=. Written once at delivery time and never modified or deleted. Authoritative provenance.
- *Per-project working copy*: =gs://chaudhuri-lab-bucket1/jeszyman/projects/<PROJECT>/inputs/<asset-class>/<local-id>/<filename>=. Scoped to one analysis project, with project-local identifiers (e.g. =lib0622/= rather than =Sample_95_nf1_lib95/=) but the same leaf filenames as the raw delivery for traceability. Bucket-internal =gsutil cp= = server-side, no compute, no egress, no rewriting.
Rationale: the raw archive can never be corrupted by an analysis pipeline, and any project can be wholly torn down (or its inputs re-pulled from scratch) without touching the raw layer. Forward policy: when a new vendor delivery lands at the raw path, mint local IDs in =sequencing_libraries=/=cfdna_isolations= and run a bucket-internal =gsutil -m cp -n= to populate the project's =inputs/= mirror before any analysis host pulls a working copy.

Stores in use:
- =jeff-beast:/mnt/data/projects/nf1-mouse/= — local working store (heavy intermediates, 130 GB)
- =/mnt/gcs/jeszyman/projects/nf1-mouse/= — GCS bucket =chaudhuri-lab-bucket1= (source-of-truth inputs + production analysis outputs)
- =big-boy:/mnt/data/projects/nf1-mouse/= — ephemeral GCE VM (3 TB local disk, currently 98% full; spun up for full-depth alignment of all 18 Run-1 libraries, torn down after outputs are rsynced to bucket)
- =gs://chaudhuri-lab-bucket1/jeszyman/data/nf1/Project_JohnShern_CS039619_112EMseqlib_061225/= — source-of-truth raw FASTQs (Run 1, nf1_lib1–lib112; Run-1 mouse libs are nf1_lib95–nf1_lib112 = lib0622–lib0639). =emseq/fastqs/= working copies derive from here.
- jeff-pad, jeff-ubuntu8 — not analysis hosts (Syncthing of org files only / small one-off jobs); no nf1-mouse data tree

*Design principle 3 — workflow data flow must not touch the FUSE bucket mount.* All inter-store transfers use =gsutil rsync= (or =gsutil cp -r= with explicit =gs://= URIs), never =cp= to/from =/mnt/gcs/=. This extends to /reads/ as well as writes: snakemake rules, pipeline scripts, and any automated data path must use a local working copy (localized first via =gsutil -m cp -r=) rather than dereferencing through the FUSE mount. The mount is for interactive one-off human browsing only. Rationale: FUSE-mediated reads have caused silent partial reads, hangs, and surprise latency in prior pipeline runs; gsutil-localized copies are checksum-verified and reliable.

*** Layout-consistency notes
- *Path-canonical violation at =ref/biscuit/=*: beast and bucket both have a =ref/biscuit/= directory, but the subdirectories underneath are different references (=icrg_hg38_mm10 + mm10= on beast vs =ncbi_hg38_noalt + ucsc_mm10= in bucket). Scripts that resolve =ref/biscuit/<name>= via a host-prefix swap will silently behave differently. Fix: rename to =ref/biscuit/pilot/{icrg_hg38_mm10,mm10}= on beast and =ref/biscuit/production/{ncbi_hg38_noalt,ucsc_mm10}= on both, or drop the pilot refs from beast since they're no longer the canonical references.
- *Production analysis lives on the bucket*: the sub-50M production tree (=emseq/pdx-read-handling-sub50M/=) is bucket-only. The beast counterpart =emseq/cna-sub50M-rerun/= is a rerun of selected libs with different ichorCNA configs (=nopon + withpon=) and lives only on beast — it sits at a different canonical path, which is correct.
- *Raw FASTQs are beast-only*: =emseq/fastqs/= holds only lib0625 + lib0626 PE150 reads. All other libs (lib0622, lib0623, lib0624, lib0627, sub-50M cohort) are accessed from already-aligned BAMs in the bucket, not re-aligned locally.
- *Empty skeleton on beast*: =data/=, =inputs/=, =qc/=, =tmp/= exist as empty placeholders. This is intentional canonical-layout discipline so scripts can mkdir into known locations even when no data lives there yet.

#!/usr/bin/env bash
set -euo pipefail
# Strategy 3: Disambiguate via dual BISCUIT alignment
# Env: emseq (biscuit + samtools)
# BISCUIT NM tag = true mismatches only (conversion in ZC tag)

SAMPLE=$1
THREADS=${2:-8}
HG38_REF="/mnt/data/projects/nf1-mouse/ref/biscuit/ncbi_hg38_noalt/GCA_000001405.15_GRCh38_no_alt_analysis_set.fna"
MM10_REF="/mnt/data/projects/nf1-mouse/ref/biscuit/ucsc_mm10/mm10.fa"
FASTQ_DIR="/mnt/data/projects/nf1-mouse/emseq/fastqs/${SAMPLE}"
OUTDIR="/mnt/data/projects/nf1-mouse/emseq/pdx-read-handling/strategy3/${SAMPLE}"
mkdir -p "$OUTDIR"

R1=$(ls "${FASTQ_DIR}"/*_R1_001.fastq.gz)
R2=$(ls "${FASTQ_DIR}"/*_R2_001.fastq.gz)

# Align to human
biscuit align -@ "$THREADS" "$HG38_REF" "$R1" "$R2" \
  | samtools sort -@ "$THREADS" -o "${OUTDIR}/${SAMPLE}.hg38.sorted.bam"
samtools index "${OUTDIR}/${SAMPLE}.hg38.sorted.bam"

# Align to mouse
biscuit align -@ "$THREADS" "$MM10_REF" "$R1" "$R2" \
  | samtools sort -@ "$THREADS" -o "${OUTDIR}/${SAMPLE}.mm10.sorted.bam"
samtools index "${OUTDIR}/${SAMPLE}.mm10.sorted.bam"

# Disambiguate: compare NM tags, assign to species with fewer mismatches
python3 "$(dirname "$0")/disambiguate.py" \
  --human-bam "${OUTDIR}/${SAMPLE}.hg38.sorted.bam" \
  --mouse-bam "${OUTDIR}/${SAMPLE}.mm10.sorted.bam" \
  --out-prefix "${OUTDIR}/${SAMPLE}" \
  --threads "$THREADS"

samtools flagstat "${OUTDIR}/${SAMPLE}.hg38.sorted.bam" > "${OUTDIR}/${SAMPLE}.hg38.flagstat.txt"
samtools flagstat "${OUTDIR}/${SAMPLE}.mm10.sorted.bam" > "${OUTDIR}/${SAMPLE}.mm10.flagstat.txt"
samtools flagstat "${OUTDIR}/${SAMPLE}.disambig.human.bam" > "${OUTDIR}/${SAMPLE}.disambig.human.flagstat.txt"

*** Results
**** ICRG combined reference failure
ICRG (concatenated hg38+mm10 reference, chromosome filtering) achieves 99.9% accuracy for WES, RRBS, and RNA-seq [cite:@callari2018] but fails catastrophically for whole-genome EM-seq. In two WU-487 terminal bleed PDX samples (lib0625, lib0626; 3M read pairs each), ICRG assigned <1% of reads to human chromosomes despite Disambiguate identifying 17-20% human content.

The failure has two components: (1) mouse contigs score 1-3 points higher than human orthologs for ~30% of ambiguous reads, and (2) among reads with identical alignment scores (AS==XS), 96% are assigned to mouse chromosomes rather than the expected ~50% from random tie-breaking. Both effects correlate with bisulfite conversion count (ZC tag) — reads with heavy C→T conversion are almost exclusively routed to mouse.

The likely mechanism is that EM-seq C→T conversion at unmethylated CpGs reduces the effective sequence alphabet, collapsing species-distinguishing variants at orthologous loci. RRBS avoids this by targeting only CpG-island-enriched fragments (~1-3% of genome). ICRG is not viable for whole-genome EM-seq PDX read handling; Disambiguate with BISCUIT (conversion-corrected NM tags) is the recommended approach.

*** Background

Patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models engraft human tumor tissue in immunocompromised mice, enabling translational studies of circulating cell-free DNA (cfDNA) as a biomarker for tumor monitoring. However, cfDNA isolated from PDX mouse plasma contains a mixture of human tumor-derived and mouse host-derived fragments. Because approximately 85% of the mouse and human genomes share sequence homology, mouse-origin reads can map to the human reference with high alignment scores and pass standard quality filters (MAPQ >= 20), creating systematic contamination in downstream copy number analysis.

This notebook evaluates three PDX read handling strategies for cfDNA copy number alteration (CNA) analysis using 50-million read-pair subsets from 6 WU-487 MPNST terminal bleeds. Strategy 1 (S1) aligns reads directly to hg38 without species filtering. Strategy 3A (S3A) uses NM-tag-based disambiguation [cite:@ahdesmaki2017] to retain only reads that align better to the human than mouse reference. Strategy 3B (S3B) extends S3A by including ambiguous reads (those with equal alignment scores to both references). All strategies use the BISCUIT aligner [cite:@zhou2024] uniformly to avoid confounding by aligner choice [cite:@bhandari2025]. Tumor fraction is estimated using ichorCNA [cite:@adalsteinsson2017].

Disambiguation classifies reads by comparing the NM tag (number of true mismatches, excluding C-to-T conversion events) between hg38 and mm10 alignments for each read pair. BISCUIT separates conversion mismatches (ZC tag) from true mismatches (NM tag), making the NM comparison unbiased by methylation state — a critical requirement for EM-seq data. Reads with fewer mismatches to hg38 are classified as human; fewer to mm10 as mouse; equal NM values as ambiguous. No minimum NM difference threshold is applied — a single-mismatch advantage is sufficient for classification.

The WU-487 PDX line is an NF1-associated malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor (MPNST) characterized by biallelic NF1 loss and PRC2 inactivation (SUZ12 loss), resulting in loss of H3K27me3 [cite:@heidrich2022]. This line is Chr8q-WT (no Chr8q gain), providing a negative control for CNA detection at that locus. Key MPNST CNA loci include CDKN2A/B deletion (9p21), TP53 alteration (17p13), NF1 loss (17q11), and PRC2 component deletions (SUZ12 at 17q11, EED at 11q14, EZH2 at 7q36).

Disambiguation removes mouse contamination but does not enrich for human tumor content — it simply reveals whatever human DNA is present, which in low-TF samples is mostly non-cfDNA.

**** Key findings

1. *Mouse contamination dominates S1*: 67-71% of reads are mouse-origin, and 68.6% of S1 MAPQ >= 20 reads in lib0626 are mouse contamination. Direct alignment to hg38 without species filtering is unsuitable for PDX cfDNA CNA analysis.

2. *Disambiguation reveals true tumor content*: S3A tumor fractions range from 0.05 to 0.65, compared to 0.07-0.38 for S1. The 2 high-TF samples (lib0626, lib0627) show TF increases of 5.8x and 1.7x respectively after disambiguation.

3. *CNA profiles improve with disambiguation*: Genome-wide CNA profiles for high-TF samples show clear gains and losses only after disambiguation. S1 profiles are flat due to signal dilution by mouse contamination.

4. *PoN improves S1 more than disambiguated strategies*: The panel of normals corrects reference-specific artifacts that overlap with contamination effects, providing a larger improvement for S1 (CV 0.33 to 0.11) than for S3A (CV 0.30 to 0.11).

5. *Fragment length profiles confirm cfDNA after disambiguation*: Only high-TF samples show a mono-nucleosomal peak after disambiguation, validating that S3A enriches for true cfDNA.

6. *Tumor volume alone does not explain TF variation*: Despite a 2.4-fold range in tumor volume, the vehicle arm shows uniformly low TF. This suggests that factors beyond bulk tumor size — such as tumor vascularity, necrosis, or treatment-induced apoptosis — contribute to cfDNA shedding. The two high-TF samples are both from the mirdametinib arm; whether MEK inhibition promotes tumor cell death and cfDNA release in WU-487 cannot be determined at n=3 per arm but warrants investigation.

**** Production pipeline recommendation

Based on these findings, the production pipeline should use:
- *Disambiguate (S3A)*: Human-only reads with NM-tag comparison
- *BISCUIT aligner* uniformly for both human and mouse reference alignments
- *ichorCNA default-low-input preset with PoN* for tumor fraction estimation
- *Autosomes only* (chrX excluded to avoid sex-chromosome artifacts)

S3B (including ambiguous reads) provides marginally more coverage but dilutes tumor signal; S3A is preferred for CNA analysis where signal purity outweighs depth.

*** Limitations

1. *Small sample size (n = 6)*: Statistical power is limited for correlation analyses. The Spearman correlation between tumor volume and TF is not significant, but the biological trend is consistent with expectations.

2. *Single PDX line*: All samples are from WU-487 (Chr8q-WT). Validation on Chr8q-gain lines is needed to confirm that disambiguation correctly identifies this CNA.

3. *Subsampled data*: 50M read-pair subsets may underestimate CNA detection sensitivity at low TF. Production data with full sequencing depth (~380M pairs) may reveal additional signal.

4. *No deduplication*: The 0% duplicate rate in subsampled data is artificial. Production runs will have PCR duplicates that reduce effective coverage.

5. *EM-seq treated as WGS*: BISCUIT alignment handles methylation conversion, but ichorCNA does not account for methylation-driven read depth biases (e.g., methylation-dependent coverage variation at CpG islands).

6. *Default hg38 PoN*: The PoN was built from non-PDX WGS samples and may not fully correct for PDX-specific artifacts. A PDX-specific PoN could further improve accuracy.

7. *No orthogonal TF validation*: Tumor fraction is estimated solely by ichorCNA (CNA-based). Orthogonal measurements such as dPCR for tumor-specific mutations, mutation VAF from targeted sequencing, or LINE-1 species-specific qPCR would strengthen confidence in the TF estimates, particularly for the low-TF samples where ichorCNA operates near its detection limit.

7. *Implantation time confound*: Mirdametinib mice were collected 9 days later than vehicle (day 72 vs day 63 post-implant). Larger terminal tumor volumes in the mirdametinib arm may reflect additional growth time rather than treatment resistance. Longitudinal growth curves should be examined to distinguish these interpretations.

8. *Fragment enrichment is TF-dependent*: Disambiguation enriches the cfDNA nucleosomal peak only in high-TF samples (23-28% of reads in 150-180 bp region for S3A vs 19% for S1). In low-TF samples, S3A actually shows a LOWER peak fraction (3-7%) than S1 (15-17%), because human reads surviving disambiguation are predominantly host genomic DNA rather than tumor cfDNA.

*** Methylation-aware Xengsort for EM-seq PDX read classification [#Y]
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       A1835C32-1FEB-4902-8127-DCF343353E83
:END:
Xengsort uses k-mer hashing to classify reads as human or mouse before alignment. It was designed for unconverted WGS/WES/RNA-seq data. For EM-seq, enzymatic C-to-T conversion at unmethylated cytosines breaks k-mer identity, pushing reads into "ambiguous" or "neither" bins.

Most k-mers (those without CpGs) are unaffected by conversion and may carry enough signal for correct classification. However, CpG-dense regions (islands, shores) — which are the most informative for methylation analysis — are disproportionately affected.

Potential approach: for each k-mer in the Xengsort index that contains CpG sites, enumerate the methylation conversion variants (C or T at each CpG position) and insert all variants with the same species label. A 25-mer with N CpGs produces 2^N variants. For most k-mers (0-1 CpGs) this is 1-2 entries; for CpG-dense k-mers (2-3 CpGs) it's 4-8 entries. Hash table expansion is modest since CpG-dense regions are a small fraction of the genome.

Key questions:
- How much does the hash table expand with full CpG enumeration?
- At what CpG density per k-mer does the combinatorial expansion become impractical?
- Can a partial enumeration (e.g., cap at 2 CpGs per k-mer, dump higher to ambiguous) balance precision and compute?
- Does this outperform Disambiguate on real EM-seq data, or does Disambiguate's alignment-score comparison remain superior?

For the current PDX read handling evaluation, Xengsort is dropped in favor of Disambiguate (alignment-score comparison handles conversion naturally via bwa-meth). This idea is for potential future tool development.
